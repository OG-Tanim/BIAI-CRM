# BIAI CRM — Enquiry Capture and Pipeline

## Problem Statement

People reach BIAI through WhatsApp, Facebook Messenger, Facebook comments, the website form and phone calls. Each message arrives as unstructured text — sometimes English, sometimes Banglish, often Bangla script — and someone has to read it, work out who the person is, what they want, and whether they are already a customer.

Today that work happens in someone's head and in scattered chat threads. The consequences are concrete:

- **Enquiries get lost.** A message that arrives while the team is busy is never answered, and nobody notices it is missing.
- **Nobody can see who needs a follow-up.** There is no single view of who has been contacted, who replied, who has paid, and who has gone quiet for a week.
- **Typing details is slow.** Transcribing name, phone, channel and intent by hand for every message is minutes per enquiry, and the fields end up inconsistent.
- **Replies are written from scratch.** Every reply is composed from nothing, in whichever language the staff member happens to reach for, which produces replies that do not match how the customer wrote.
- **Non-enquiries and real money are indistinguishable.** A crypto spam comment, a job seeker, and a customer saying "I paid but got no confirmation" all land in the same inbox with no way to tell them apart.
- **Corporate leads are not distinguished from students.** A managing director of a textile company and a school student arrive looking almost identical once the sender's title and company are discarded.

The people doing this work are an enterprise sales team sharing one board. V1 therefore assumes a trusted internal audience sitting behind the company's existing access controls: no login, no user accounts, and no per-person attribution.

## Solution

A single-page CRM in which a team member pastes a raw enquiry message and gets two independent AI assists. **Read message** returns an editable form: name, company, job title, phone, channel, channel handle, what the person wants, what kind of message it is, a one-line summary and an attention flag. **Generate reply** drafts a separate reply in the same language the person used, with prices, dates and policies left as clearly marked fill-in placeholders. The same form and reply can be filled in by hand from the start; the AI assists are optional, not gates.

The reply is one editable box, not a template beside a second filled copy. It can be generated by **Generate reply** or written by hand. **Read message** fills the enquiry details only and leaves the reply box alone; Generate reply touches only the reply box, and either way the box can be copied at any time.

Nothing is saved until the operator reviews it. The operator corrects anything the AI got wrong, presses save, and the enquiry lands on a pipeline board in the **New** column.

From there the operator works the board: move a card to **Contacted** when they reach out, **Interested** when the person asks a buying question, **Paid** once money is actually verified, or **Lost** when the deal is dead. Every status change can carry a note. The board shows five columns, a count per column, and how long each card has been sitting there.

Two supporting views fall out of the same data: a **follow-up queue** listing enquiries in New or Contacted that nobody has touched for 48 hours, and a **weekly summary** generated by the AI from an aggregate of the week's activity.

Non-sales messages — spam, job seekers, support issues — are captured and classified but never appear as sales cards on the board or in the follow-up queue. They stay visible in a list so nothing is silently deleted.

The AI is an assistant, not an actor. It cannot mark anything Paid, it cannot create a duplicate without warning the operator, and it cannot invent a phone number.

## User Stories

### Capturing an enquiry

1. As a sales team member, I want to paste a raw enquiry message into a form, so that I do not have to retype the person's details by hand.
2. As a sales team member, I want the AI to prefill name, company, job title, phone, channel, interest, kind, summary and suggested reply from the pasted message, so that capture takes seconds instead of minutes.
3. As a sales team member, I want every prefilled field to be editable before saving, so that an AI mistake never reaches the database unchecked.
4. As a sales team member, I want nothing written to the database until I press save, so that abandoned or mistaken extractions leave no junk records.
5. As a sales team member, I want a newly saved enquiry to start in the New status, so that the board always reflects what I have actually done rather than what the AI guessed.
6. As a sales team member, I want the channel recorded as WhatsApp, Facebook, website or phone, so that I know where to reply and how the person found us.
7. As a sales team member, I want phone to be optional, so that Facebook DMs and comments without a number can still be captured.
8. As a sales team member, I want an optional channel handle (WhatsApp username, email address, Facebook profile), so that people with no phone number are still reachable.
9. As a sales team member, I want the AI to record what the person wants as bootcamp, fellowship, corporate training or unclear, so that I can route and quote correctly.
10. As a sales team member, I want the AI to classify each message as a sales lead, job seeker, support issue, spam or unclear, so that non-sales messages do not masquerade as leads.
11. As a sales team member, I want a one-line summary of the message, so that board cards are readable without opening each one.
12. As a sales team member, I want the original raw message stored verbatim and never overwritten, so that I can always check what was actually said.
13. As a sales team member, I want the sender's job title and company split into their own fields rather than deleted, so that I can tell who has budget authority before I call.

### Answering

14. As a sales team member, I want a suggested reply drafted from the message, so that I do not start from a blank page.
15. As a sales team member, I want the suggested reply in the same language the person wrote in, so that the reply does not read as a canned foreign-language response.
16. As a sales team member, I want a message written in Bangla script to get a Bangla reply, so that the reply matches how the person wrote.
17. As a sales team member, I want a message written in Banglish to get a Banglish reply, so that the reply matches how the person wrote.
18. As a sales team member, I want a message written in English to get an English reply, so that the reply matches how the person wrote.
19. As a sales team member, I want to copy the suggested reply with one click, so that I can paste it into WhatsApp or Messenger without selecting text.
20. As a sales team member, I want a reply that addresses the actual question asked (price, schedule, instalments, refunds, certificate), so that I am not sending a generic greeting.

### Working the pipeline

21. As a sales team member, I want a board with the five columns New, Contacted, Interested, Paid and Lost, so that I can see the state of every deal at a glance.
22. As a sales team member, I want each card to show the person's name, company, job title, interest, kind and age, so that I can triage without opening it.
23. As a sales team member, I want to move a card between statuses, so that the board tracks what I have actually done.
24. As a sales team member, I want to attach a short note whenever I change a status, so that I remember what was said and when.
25. As a sales team member, I want to see how long since the last activity on each card, so that I know which deals are going stale.
26. As a sales team member, I want a count of enquiries per column, so that I can see the shape of the pipeline instantly.
27. As a sales team member, I want an explicit "needs attention" flag on a card, so that urgent cases stand out without being given a fake status.

### Excluding non-leads

28. As a sales team member, I want spam classified as spam and kept off the board entirely, so that the pipeline is not polluted by bots.
29. As a sales team member, I want job seekers captured but kept out of the sales pipeline, so that I can still answer them without them becoming fake deals.
30. As a sales team member, I want support issues captured and flagged rather than discarded, so that a customer saying "I paid and got no confirmation" is never lost.
31. As a sales team member, I want an angry or threatening message to raise the attention flag automatically, so that it is dealt with before it turns into a public complaint.
32. As a sales team member, I want the AI to never set a status beyond New, so that no deal is ever marked Paid or Lost on the customer's word alone.
33. As a sales team member, I want a customer's claim of payment to leave the enquiry in Contacted, so that Paid always means money I have verified.

### Handling duplicates

34. As a sales team member, I want a non-blocking warning when I save an enquiry whose phone number already exists, so that I know I may be creating a duplicate.
35. As a sales team member, I want to save anyway, so that a repeated message from the same person can still be recorded as its own enquiry.
36. As a sales team member, I want the warning to show who the existing enquiry is, so that I can decide in one glance.

### Follow-up and reporting

37. As a sales team member, I want a list of enquiries in New or Contacted that have had no activity for 48 hours, so that I know who has been forgotten.
38. As a sales team member, I want the follow-up list measured from the last contact when there was one, and from creation when there was not, so that one person is not chased twice and another is chased never.
39. As a sales team member, I want the follow-up list to exclude spam, job seekers, and anything already Paid or Lost, so that the queue contains only work worth doing.
40. As a sales team member, I want a weekly summary written by the AI from real counts, so that I can see what came in, what moved, and what is stuck.
41. As a sales team member, I want the summary to be generated from a database aggregate rather than from the AI's memory, so that the numbers are true.

### Trust and failure

42. As a sales team member, I want the form to remain fully usable when the AI call fails, so that I can still capture the enquiry by hand.
43. As a sales team member, I want the AI to leave a field empty rather than guess a phone number, so that I never call a stranger.
44. As a sales team member, I want the company and job title preserved as data rather than dropped as noise, so that records stay both clean and complete.
45. As a sales team member, I want a staff-written phone call note to be handled differently from a customer message, so that the "reply" I am drafting is a follow-up rather than a message pretending to be the customer.
46. As a sales team member, I want realistic sample data available in a separate testing environment, so that the board, the follow-up queue and the summary can be demonstrated without putting fictional people into the live database.

### Operating it

47. As a sales team member, I want to open the app at a URL with no login, so that the whole team can start working as soon as the URL is shared.
48. As a sales team member, I want a list view filterable by status and kind, so that I can find a specific enquiry outside the board.
49. As an administrator, I want the production database to stay empty until real enquiries arrive, so that demo data never contaminates live records.
50. As an administrator, I want seeding to refuse to run against production, so that a mistyped environment variable cannot create twenty fictional customers.
51. As a sales team member, I want the AI model to be configuration rather than code, so that a retired or throttled free model does not require a redeploy.
52. As a developer handing this over, I want a README recording the decisions, the deliberate cuts and the trade-offs, so that the next person can judge the work rather than guess at it.

### Keeping the reply honest

53. As a sales team member, I want the AI to never state a price, date, duration or policy, so that no invented number ever reaches a customer.
54. As a sales team member, I want those facts left as clearly marked placeholders, so that I know exactly what to fill in before sending.
55. As a sales team member, I want to fill placeholders in the reply and have the finished text stored with the enquiry, so that the reply I actually sent is on record.
56. As a sales team member, I want a warning when a placeholder is still unfilled, so that I cannot copy `{{price}}` into WhatsApp by accident.
57. As a sales team member, I want one editable reply box rather than a template and a second filled copy, so that I edit the text I will actually send.
58. As a sales team member, I want a Generate reply button that drafts only the reply from the pasted message, so that I can get help with the reply without extracting every field.
59. As a sales team member, I want Generate reply to tell me to paste a message when the message box is empty, so that I understand why nothing was generated.
60. As a sales team member, I want to copy the reply from the same box at any time, so that I do not have to wait for a save or find a second field.

## Implementation Decisions

### Domain vocabulary

This spec uses the following terms consistently. There is no pre-existing glossary in the repo; this section establishes it.

- **Enquiry** — one saved record, derived from exactly one message. There is no conversation or thread entity.
- **Kind** — what the message *is*: `sales_lead`, `job_seeker`, `support`, `spam`, `unclear`. Orthogonal to status.
- **Interest** — what the person *wants*: `bootcamp`, `fellowship`, `corporate`, `unclear`.
- **Status** — where the *deal* is, as a record of what the operator did: `new`, `contacted`, `interested`, `paid`, `lost`. Never a description of the message.
- **Channel** — where the message came from: `whatsapp`, `facebook`, `website`, `phone`.
- **Extraction** — the AI step that turns a raw message into prefilled fields. A proposal, never a commit.
- **Card** — an enquiry rendered on the board. Only `sales_lead` enquiries become cards.
- **Follow-up queue** — enquiries in `new` or `contacted` with no activity for 48 hours.
- **Operator** — any member of the sales team using the CRM, not an authenticated identity. V1 has no accounts, so the word never implies a logged-in user.

### Architecture

- Next.js (App Router) with TypeScript, deployed to Vercel. Postgres on Neon free tier with two branches — **production** and **testing** — accessed through Drizzle ORM with `drizzle-kit push` for schema changes. No migration history, which is a deliberate trade-off.
- All AI calls are server-side through the OpenAI SDK pointed at OpenRouter's OpenAI-compatible endpoint, using free-tier models. The provider key never reaches the browser.
- The model id, base URL and API key are environment variables. Free models are retired and throttled without notice, so the model is configuration, never a constant.
- Free-tier models do not reliably support strict JSON-schema response formats, so extraction uses `response_format: { type: 'json_object' }`, with the schema stated in the prompt and Zod as the final authority on what is accepted.
- Capture's AI sits behind two ports: `AiExtractor` for a full proposal and `ReplyWriter` for a reply-only draft. Both can be replaced with a fake in tests and with canned responses when no key is available. The weekly summary keeps its separate narrator port.
- Capture is not AI-gated. The details form is editable from the first render, so an operator can type every field and save without calling the model. `POST /api/extract` is an optional autofill, `POST /api/reply` is an optional reply draft, and a failure removes only the AI text, never the form.
- Rate limits and queueing are expected on the free tier: one retry with backoff on 429 and 5xx, then a clear failure that leaves the manual form usable.
- A small header in the root layout links Capture, Pipeline and List, so no view is reachable only by typing its URL. It is shell, not a fourth view.

### Schema changes

Two tables. Enums are `text` columns with `CHECK` constraints rather than Postgres enum types, so a new value is a one-line change instead of an `ALTER TYPE` dance.

```sql
enquiries
  id                uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid()
  name              text not null
  company           text null
  job_title         text null
  phone             text null
  channel           text not null check (channel in ('whatsapp','facebook','website','phone'))
  channel_handle    text null
  interest          text not null check (interest in ('bootcamp','fellowship','corporate','unclear'))
  kind              text not null check (kind in ('sales_lead','job_seeker','support','spam','unclear'))
  status            text not null default 'new'
                    check (status in ('new','contacted','interested','paid','lost'))
  needs_attention   boolean not null default false
  summary           text not null default ''
  raw_message       text not null
  suggested_reply   text not null default ''
  reply_filled      text null
  created_at        timestamptz not null default now()
  last_contacted_at timestamptz null
  updated_at        timestamptz not null default now()

  index on (phone)
  index on (status, kind)

notes
  id          uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid()
  enquiry_id  uuid not null references enquiries(id) on delete cascade
  body        text not null
  created_at  timestamptz not null default now()
```

`status` is deliberately absent from the AI response schema. A field the model cannot emit is a lie the model cannot tell.

`suggested_reply` holds the one editable reply. `reply_filled` remains in the schema for rows saved under the earlier two-column design; new saves mirror the single reply into both columns, and the UI never presents `reply_filled` as a second box. Opening an older enquiry starts the box from `reply_filled ?? suggested_reply`, so the text actually sent is not hidden behind a newer template.

`company` and `job_title` exist so that the information in a sender name like `"Mahmud Hasan (Managing Director, Gazipur textile company)"` is preserved rather than thrown away. `name` holds the person; the role and organisation are captured beside it, not merged into it and not discarded.

### API contracts

- `POST /api/extract` — body `{ message: string }`. Returns a validated `ExtractionResult` with no reply fields. Does not write to the database.
- `POST /api/reply` — body `{ message: string }`. Returns `{ reply: string | null }`. The model's reply text is returned as-is; there is no rejection reason. Drafts only a reply; it cannot alter identity fields or status and does not write to the database. The reply language is an internal prompt decision and is not part of the response.
- `POST /api/enquiries` — body is the reviewed form fields. Always inserts with `status: 'new'`. Returns the created enquiry plus `duplicateOf` when another enquiry shares the phone number.
- `PATCH /api/enquiries/:id` — body `{ status?, note?, needs_attention?, suggested_reply?, reply_filled? }`. Applies a human status change, optionally appends a note, and edits the single reply. The client sends the one box as both `suggested_reply` and `reply_filled`; the second field stays accepted for compatibility with existing rows. No endpoint writes a reply outside its enquiry.
- `GET /api/pipeline` — returns five columns of `sales_lead` enquiries with per-column counts.
- `GET /api/reminders` — returns overdue enquiries per the 48-hour rule.
- `POST /api/summary` — aggregates the last seven days and asks the AI to narrate the numbers.

### Extraction contract

The AI returns exactly this shape, validated with Zod on the server. Any field that fails validation becomes `null` — never a repair, never a guess.

```ts
type ExtractionResult = {
  name: string;
  company: string | null;
  job_title: string | null;
  phone: string | null;
  channel: 'whatsapp' | 'facebook' | 'website' | 'phone';
  channel_handle: string | null;
  interest: 'bootcamp' | 'fellowship' | 'corporate' | 'unclear';
  kind: 'sales_lead' | 'job_seeker' | 'support' | 'spam' | 'unclear';
  summary: string;
  needs_attention: boolean;
  // no status, no suggested_reply, no reply text
};
```

Read message never produces reply text. A reply the model volunteers is stripped by the extraction schema; the reply box is filled only by Generate reply or by the operator.

### Reply-only contract

The Generate reply path returns exactly this shape, validated by `validateReplyDraft`. Any other key is stripped, so a reply request cannot carry a status or a phone number out of the model:

```ts
type ReplyDraft = {
  reply: string | null;
};
```

A null or blank reply is not an error: `reply` is `null` and the UI places an empty value in the box. The writer also tolerates a model that answers with `suggested_reply` instead of `reply`. The reply language is chosen inside the prompt; it is not returned, stored, or shown.

Extraction prompt rules, in priority order:

1. Never invent a phone number. If no number appears in the message, return `null`. Six of the twenty samples have none.
2. Never return a status. Status is set by the operator.
3. Never return a reply, `suggested_reply` or any other reply text. Reply drafting is a separate action.
4. Never infer identity from a name. A first name alone does not establish who someone is.
5. Split the sender's role and organisation out of their name instead of dropping either. `"Mahmud Hasan (Managing Director, Gazipur textile company)"` yields `name: "Mahmud Hasan"`, `job_title: "Managing Director"`, `company: "Gazipur textile company"`. Leave `job_title` and `company` null when the message does not state them — most individual students state neither.
6. A phone call note is staff-authored, not customer-authored. Classify it by its content.
7. A claim of payment is not a payment. `kind` may be `support` and `needs_attention` true, but nothing about status changes.

### Reply placeholders

The model is forbidden from asserting facts it does not have. The system holds no price list, no cohort calendar and no refund policy, so instead of writing *"the bootcamp costs 12,000 taka"* it writes *"the bootcamp costs {{price}}"*. The sales team fills the rest. A placeholder costs the operator two seconds; an invented price costs a customer.

Nine allowlisted tokens:

| Token | Fills |
|---|---|
| `{{price}}` | Course or training fee |
| `{{start_date}}` | Next cohort start date |
| `{{schedule}}` | Class days, times, live versus recorded |
| `{{location}}` | Physical venue |
| `{{instalments}}` | Whether and how payment can be split |
| `{{discount}}` | Group, student or corporate discount terms |
| `{{refund_policy}}` | Refund and cancellation terms |
| `{{certificate}}` | Certificate issuer and recognition |
| `{{payment_method}}` | Accepted payment channels |

Rules:

- The reply prompt asks for only these nine tokens. Matching is literal: `{{ price }}` is not `{{price}}`. A generated reply is not rejected for carrying another token; the operator sees it in the "Needs filling" line and copying asks for confirmation. The allowlist is enforced when an operator edits a reply.
- The reply is one editable box on capture and on the enquiry detail view. The model writes it into `suggested_reply`; the operator edits that same text before and after save.
- `reply_filled` is retained for rows saved under the earlier two-column design. New saves mirror the single reply into both columns, so the stored text and the one box cannot disagree. Opening an older enquiry starts the box from `reply_filled ?? suggested_reply`, so the text actually sent is not hidden behind a newer template.
- `hasUnfilledPlaceholder` guards the copy action: while `{{` remains, copying asks for confirmation and warns instead of silently sending `{{price}}` to a customer. Saving is still allowed — the reply is a draft until someone sends it.
- Placeholders appear only where the message actually asks for that fact. A reply to "how do I join?" carries no `{{price}}` unless money was raised.

That last rule is about tone as much as accuracy: a reply carrying six placeholders reads like a form, not a message.

### Reply generation

The reply box is filled by Generate reply or by the operator:

- **Read message** extracts the enquiry details only. It never writes the reply box, and a reply already there — typed or generated — survives a re-read.
- **Generate reply** calls `POST /api/reply` and replaces only the reply box with the returned text. Name, phone, channel, interest, kind and status are untouched. If the box already has text, the operator confirms before it is replaced.
- **No message pasted**: Generate reply sends no AI request, moves focus to the message box and explains that the message is required. The operator can still write the reply by hand.
- **Any reply the model returns**: the text is placed in the box as-is, with no rejection reason or warning. If the model returned nothing, the box is left empty.

### Reply language

The reply model owns the language decision, and that decision stays inside the prompt. The prompt instructs it to evaluate the message first — Bangla written in Bengali script, romanised Bangla, or English — then write the reply in that same language. It handles the cases a keyword list cannot: Banglish dense with English vocabulary (#17, *"Apnader course certificate ta ki govt approved?"*), a Bangla-script message quoting English product names, or a message that switches script mid-sentence.

There is no deterministic language check and no language field. The server does not inspect the message or the reply for Bengali script, does not compare a model label against the text, and does not store or return the choice; the reply the model returns is the reply the operator sees.

The capture screen has one reply box and no language selector. The enquiry detail view shows the reply text only. The brief specifies "English or Banglish", but four of the twenty samples (#2, #7, #13, #18) are written in Bangla script, so matching the sender's script is part of the prompt's internal guidance.

### Status transitions

- Status is changed only by an explicit operator action.
- Any transition out of `new` stamps `last_contacted_at = now()`. A later move leaves that time alone: it records where the deal got to, not that the person was reached again.
- `needs_attention` is the operator's own mark and moves independently of status; setting or clearing it is not a status change and stamps nothing.
- Appending a note without a status change does not move a deal or restamp contact time.
- `updated_at` records the last status move. A note-only, flag-only or reply-only update leaves it alone, so the weekly `moved` count means deals that changed stage rather than rows that were touched.
- `paid` and `lost` are reachable from any status; there is no enforced state machine beyond that, because real sales do not follow one and a first version cannot afford to argue with the operator.

### The 48-hour rule

An enquiry is overdue when:

`COALESCE(last_contacted_at, created_at) <= now() - interval '48 hours'` and `status in ('new','contacted')` and `kind = 'sales_lead'`.

Non-sales enquiries are excluded before the time comparison, not after.

### Environments and seed data

Two Neon branches, each with its own connection string, selected by environment variable. **Production ships empty** and receives only enquiries the team captures — it is never seeded, and no sample data exists in it at any point.

The seed script inserts all twenty sample enquiries with genuine timestamps spread across the sample dates, against the testing branch only. It refuses to run when its resolved target matches the production connection string, and it prints the branch it is about to write to before writing. That guard costs a few lines and prevents the worst mistake available in this design: a mistyped variable filling the live database with twenty fictional people.

The demo runs against the testing branch. Because the samples are dated 2026-10-26 and 27 and the build is reviewed later, every seeded enquiry is already overdue, so the follow-up queue is full on first load. That is expected and makes the feature visible.

## Testing Decisions

### What makes a good test here

Tests assert external behaviour only: given this input, that observable output or that row in the database. No assertions on which internal function was called, no mocking of the database, no snapshot tests of rendered markup. If a refactor moves code between modules without changing behaviour, no test should need to change.

### Seam 1 — pure domain module

A single I/O-free module holding the decisions that are easy to get subtly wrong. Tested with Vitest, no database, no network.

- `validateExtraction` — returns identity and classification only; rejects an unknown `status` key if the model emits one anyway; strips a volunteered `suggested_reply`; coerces an invalid phone to `null`; preserves `null` phone when the message has none.
- `validateReplyDraft` — accepts only the reply (or a `suggested_reply` alias); strips a model-supplied status, phone or language; returns the model's reply unchanged, including any placeholder.
- `isDueForFollowUp` — false at 47h59m, true at exactly 48h, true beyond; false for `paid`, `lost`, `spam`, `job_seeker`; true when `last_contacted_at` is stale even if `created_at` is recent.
- `applyStatusChange` — `new → contacted` stamps `last_contacted_at`; a note-only update does not; a later move keeps the first contact time; the attention flag flips independently of status; `paid` is reachable only via this function, never from extraction.
- `applyReplyEdit` — edits the template and the filled text independently; rejects a template token outside the allowlist; accepts an empty filled version; drops a stale filled version when the template changes without one supplied.
- `assertPlaceholdersAllowed` — rejects `{{cohort_two}}` because it is not on the allowlist, and `{{ price }}` because matching is literal; accepts all nine allowlisted tokens, including repeated use of one token.
- `hasUnfilledPlaceholder` — true for `"the fee is {{price}}"`, false once every token has been replaced.

Language choice itself is not unit-tested, because it is the model's judgement. The tests assert that a model reply survives unchanged rather than checking its script; the placeholder allowlist is tested as the manual-edit guard.

### Seam 2 — HTTP routes

Route handlers tested against a throwaway Postgres database, with the AI ports replaced by fakes that return fixtures. These cover the wiring that a pure-function suite cannot: does the pipeline query actually exclude spam, does the reminder query actually join the right columns, does the save endpoint actually force `status: 'new'`.

- `POST /api/extract` returns a complete prefilled payload with no reply fields and writes nothing.
- `POST /api/reply` returns only a reply, rejects a missing message without calling the AI, and writes nothing.
- `POST /api/enquiries` creates exactly one row with `status: 'new'` and returns `duplicateOf` on a repeat phone.
- `PATCH /api/enquiries/:id` moves a card, appends the note, and saves an edited single reply.
- `GET /api/pipeline` returns five columns containing only `sales_lead` rows.
- `GET /api/reminders` excludes non-sales and resolved enquiries.

### Prior art

There is none — this is a greenfield repository with no application code, no test runner and no ADRs. These tests establish the pattern that later work should follow: pure decisions tested directly, wiring tested at the route boundary, nothing tested through the UI.

## Out of Scope

- Authentication, user accounts, roles, per-user attribution, or multi-tenancy. V1 is internal, situated behind the company's existing access controls, and trusts the team. Notes are unattributed by design, because there is nobody to attribute them to.
- Conversation threads. A repeat message from the same person is a separate enquiry; the only concession is a non-blocking duplicate warning.
- Bulk CSV import and any file upload. The twenty samples are loaded by a seed script into the testing branch only.
- Live ingestion from WhatsApp, Facebook or the website — no webhooks, no API integrations, no polling. Messages are pasted by hand.
- Payment verification, invoicing, or integration with any gateway. "Paid" is a human assertion.
- Edit history or audit log beyond the notes table.
- Rich text, attachments, images, or voice notes in the raw message.
- Internationalisation of the interface, theming, dark mode, mobile layout.
- Analytics, funnels, conversion reporting beyond the weekly summary.
- Automated deployment pipelines, observability, or error tracking.
- AI extraction of anything other than the listed fields — no sentiment scoring, no lead scoring, no revenue forecasting.
- A price list, cohort calendar or policy knowledge base. V1 deliberately has none; replies carry placeholders precisely because the system does not know the answers.
- Model selection, benchmarking or fallback chains across providers. One model id from configuration, one retry.

## Further Notes

### Anomalies in the sample data

The twenty samples are not twenty sales leads, and the spec is shaped around that. Four rows are not leads:

| Row | What it actually is | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| #7 | Paid but no confirmation | `kind = support`, `needs_attention = true`, stays `contacted` |
| #11 | Crypto spam | `kind = spam`, never reaches the board or the queue |
| #16 | Cancel and refund, threatening to post publicly | `kind = support`, `needs_attention = true`, `lost` after handling |
| #20 | Job applicant | `kind = job_seeker`, not `lost` — they never said no |

Other rows that will break a naive extractor:

- #3 and #10 carry a job title and company inside the sender name, which is exactly the case `company` and `job_title` exist for.
- #18 names a company and a role in a different shape — *"Rezaul Karim (owner, small garment factory)"* — and should yield `job_title: "owner"`, `company: "small garment factory"`.
- #5 is a staff-written phone note, not a customer message.
- #1 and #12 are the same person, two days apart.
- #2, #6, #9, #11, #13, #17 have no phone number at all.
- #6 and #11 arrive as Facebook comments, where a private reply is not possible.

### Demo script

Six beats, roughly three minutes, run against the testing branch:

1. Paste #1, press extract, fields fill, save — a New card appears.
2. Paste #3 — company and job title populate, so a corporate lead is visibly different from a student.
3. Paste #11 — classified spam, and it does not appear on the board.
4. Paste #16 — classified support with the attention flag, top of the queue.
5. Paste #2 — Bangla in, Bangla reply out.
6. Open Rafi's enquiry from the list, fill `{{price}}` and `{{payment_method}}` in the single reply box, save it, then copy — copying asks for confirmation while any placeholder is still unfilled.

### Build order

Scope is set by value rather than ambition, and work proceeds in dependency order: scaffold and deploy, extraction route, capture form, board, follow-up queue, weekly summary, test data, README. If the weekly summary turns out to be the weakest remaining piece, cut it and document the cut rather than shipping it half-built.

### Known risks

- **Free-model throttling at demo time.** OpenRouter free models are rate-limited, queue unexpectedly, and are occasionally withdrawn. Mitigation: one retry with backoff, `MOCK_AI` canned responses keyed to the sample messages, and a visible banner rather than a spinner that never resolves.
- **No API key at review time.** The AI features silently do nothing, which reads as broken. Mitigation: `MOCK_AI` mode, or a banner stating that AI is disabled and manual entry is required.
- **Structured-output drift.** Free models sometimes wrap JSON in prose or emit a field the schema forbids. Zod rejects; anything unparseable is a failed extraction, not a partial write.
- **Cold starts.** The Neon free tier can add a second or two to the first query; the first screen should not appear hung.
- **Extraction latency.** A single message should extract in a few seconds; a spinner is required, and the save button must remain disabled until the form is populated.
- **Placeholder fatigue.** A model that reaches for placeholders too eagerly produces replies that read like forms. Mitigation: the prompt emits a placeholder only where the message asks for that fact, and writes natural prose around it.

### Tooling gap

No issue tracker or triage label vocabulary is configured in this repository — `/setup-matt-pocock-skills` has not been run — so this spec lives in the repository as `docs/SPEC.md` and has not been published, and no `ready-for-agent` label has been applied.
